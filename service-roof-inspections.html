Why it matters

What we find on roofs every day

Most roof failures start small and quiet. These are the defects our crews find over and over on commercial roofs across California & Nevada.

Ponding water & clogged drains

Standing water accelerates membrane aging and adds structural load. Blocked drains and scuppers are the most common — and most fixable — problem we see.

Failed pipe flashings

Boots and flashings around pipes and penetrations crack, shrink, and pull away long before the field membrane fails.

Open seams & laps

Seams are where roofs leak first. Heat cycling and movement slowly open welds and laps that look fine from a distance.

Deteriorated sealant joints

Sealant at terminations, counterflashings, and joints has a shorter life than the roof itself — and it rarely gets renewed on time.

Punctures from rooftop traffic

HVAC service calls, dropped tools, and foot traffic leave punctures nobody reports until water shows up inside.

Temporary patches past their life

Mastic smears and quick fixes from years ago are still up there, quietly failing. We find them, document them, and repair them right.

You can't fix what you can't see. An inspection turns unknowns into a plan.

Our process

Inspect → Document → Recommend → Plan

1. Inspect

A Kodiak technician walks the entire roof — membrane, flashings, penetrations, drainage, and edge details.

2. Document

Every issue is photographed and located, so you see exactly what we see — no take-our-word-for-it.

3. Recommend

You receive a written condition report with findings prioritized: what needs attention now, what can wait, and what to watch.

4. Plan

We help you turn the report into a plan — targeted repairs, a maintenance program, or a budget timeline for eventual replacement.

Timing

When to schedule an inspection

Any time is better than after the leak — but some moments matter more than others.

  • After storms — wind and hail damage is often invisible from the ground and gets worse with every rain.
  • Before winter — the wet season is when small defects become active leaks. Get ahead of it.
  • Before warranty expiry — document covered defects while the manufacturer is still on the hook.
  • At purchase or lease — know the true condition of the roof before you sign, not after.

Questions

Roof inspection FAQ

How long does an inspection take?

It depends on the size and complexity of the roof — most commercial inspections are completed in a single visit, and we coordinate access around your operations.

Will it disrupt my building or tenants?

No. Inspections are non-invasive — we work on the roof, not inside your space, and we coordinate rooftop access with your building team.

What do I actually get afterward?

A written condition report with photo documentation of every finding and prioritized recommendations — a document you can use for budgeting, warranty claims, or due diligence.

Does an inspection obligate me to repairs?

Never. The report is yours. If repairs make sense, we'll quote them — but the inspection stands on its own with no strings attached.

Commercial roofing is dangerous work. Doing it safely — every job, every day — is the first measure of a professional contractor, and it's been our standard since 1992.

Our philosophy

Safety before schedule. Every time.

Roofing consistently ranks among the most hazardous trades in construction. We don't treat that as a cost of doing business — we treat it as the problem our planning exists to solve. Every Kodiak project starts with a safety plan, and no schedule pressure ever overrides it.

That commitment isn't just for our crews. A contractor with a disciplined safety culture is a contractor whose jobsite on your property is controlled, documented, and predictable — from staging and fall protection to how we work around your tenants, equipment, and daily operations.

Our safety program

Discipline you can see on the roof

Safety at Kodiak isn't a binder on a shelf — it's a daily practice with named responsibilities on every job.

  • Daily jobsite safety briefings before any crew member steps on the roof
  • Fall-protection systems — guardrails, anchors, and personal fall-arrest equipment — matched to each roof's conditions
  • OSHA-aligned training for every field employee, refreshed on a recurring schedule
  • A documented, site-specific safety plan written for every project before work begins
  • Unannounced site audits by supervisors to verify the plan is followed, not just filed
  • Stop-work authority for every crew member — anyone can halt the job over an unsafe condition

FAQ

Safety questions owners ask us

Can you provide safety documentation for our contractor prequalification?

Yes. We can provide our written safety program, training records, insurance certificates, and license information (CA #1119594 / CA #732770 / NV #0042603) as part of your vendor onboarding or prequalification process — just ask when you request a quote.

How do you protect our tenants and employees while work is underway?

The site-specific safety plan covers more than the roof — it defines ground-level barricades and signage, controlled zones under work areas, material staging and hoisting routes, and coordination with your facilities team so building occupants are kept clear of any hazard.

What happens if your crew finds an unsafe condition on our roof?

Work pauses, the condition is documented, and you're notified the same day with photos and a recommended fix. Whether it's deteriorated decking, an unguarded skylight, or an electrical hazard, we won't work around a danger without a plan — and we won't leave you unaware of one.

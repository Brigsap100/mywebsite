Multifamily Roofing

Apartments, condominiums, and HOA communities — roofing and waterproofing that protects residents, reserves, and property values.

The challenge

The specific demands of multifamily roofs

People live under these roofs around the clock. That changes everything about how the work has to be planned, communicated, and performed.

01

Residents in place, all day

Work happens over occupied homes — noise, parking, pets, and privacy all matter. Clear notices, predictable crew hours, and courteous conduct aren't extras; they're the job.

02

Balconies & decks

Elevated walkways, balconies, and decks are the highest-liability waterproofing on the property. Failed coatings and flashings lead to dry rot, structural damage, and inspection findings.

03

HOA board decision cycles

Boards meet monthly, vote on reserves, and answer to homeowners. They need clear condition reports, apples-to-apples options, and a contractor who can present to the board — not just bid.

04

Phased, multi-building sites

Garden-style communities spread across dozens of buildings built in phases, often with different roof ages and systems. One community can need repair, restoration, and replacement all at once.

05

Interior damage escalates fast

A leak over a unit means damaged belongings, displaced residents, and habitability claims. Response time and documentation matter as much as the repair itself.

06

Reserve-study budgeting

Roof replacement is usually the largest line in the reserve study. Owners and boards need honest remaining-life assessments so funding matches reality — no surprises.

Our approach

How Kodiak supports multifamily communities

We treat every community like the neighborhood it is. Projects are phased building by building with resident notices ahead of each move, protected walkways and parking, and daily cleanup — so life at the property goes on undisturbed.

  • Building-by-building phasing with advance resident notification

  • Balcony, deck, and walkway waterproofing alongside the roof work
  • Board-ready condition reports, photo documentation, and reserve-planning input

Systems

Roof systems we recommend for multifamily

Multifamily portfolios mix low-slope and steep-slope roofs, occupied decks, and buildings of different vintages. We match the system to each building — not the other way around.

TPO single-ply

Reflective, energy-efficient membrane for flat and low-slope buildings, carports, and clubhouses. Fast, clean installation keeps disruption to residents short.

EPDM single-ply

Time-tested rubber membrane with excellent flexibility for buildings that move and settle — a durable, economical choice for low-slope multifamily roofs.

Built-up roofing (BUR)

Redundant multi-ply protection for flat roofs that see regular foot traffic from maintenance staff, satellite and HVAC service, and amenity access.

Coatings & restoration

For occupied communities, fluid-applied restoration renews a serviceable roof without tear-off — less noise, less debris, lower cost, and a meaningful bridge while reserves build toward full replacement.

Questions

Multifamily roofing FAQs

How do you keep residents informed during a project?

We provide notice before work reaches each building — what's happening, when, where to park, and who to call. Crews keep predictable hours, protect walkways, and clean the site daily so the community stays livable throughout.

Can you present options to our HOA board?

Yes. We deliver written condition assessments with photos, remaining-life estimates, and side-by-side repair, restoration, and replacement options — and we'll walk your board or reserve-study consultant through them so the vote is an informed one.

Do you handle balconies and decks, or just roofs?

Both. Waterproofing is in our name — elevated decks, balconies, and walkways are among the most failure-prone assemblies on a multifamily property, and we waterproof them alongside the roof so nothing falls between trades.

Can a large community be re-roofed in phases across budget years?

Absolutely. We prioritize buildings by condition, phase the work to match your reserve funding, and keep the remaining roofs watertight with maintenance and repairs until their phase comes up.

Roof Systems

TPO Roofing Systems

A reflective, heat-welded single-ply membrane — one of the most widely installed low-slope roof systems in the West, and often the right answer for large commercial buildings.

The basics

What is TPO?

TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin) is a single-ply membrane: wide sheets of flexible, typically white roofing material rolled out over insulation and fastened or adhered to the deck. What makes TPO different from older systems is the seam — adjoining sheets are fused together with hot-air welding, so the finished roof behaves like one continuous, monolithic membrane rather than a patchwork of overlaps.

Because the seams are welded rather than glued or taped, they are generally the strongest part of the system when installed correctly — and the weakest when they're not. That's why who installs your TPO roof matters as much as which membrane you buy.

A typical TPO assembly includes the structural deck, one or more layers of rigid insulation, an optional cover board, the membrane itself, and welded flashings at every penetration, wall, and edge.

Best-use cases

Where TPO excels

TPO earned its market share honestly. On the right building it delivers reflectivity, speed of installation, and clean detailing at a competitive cost.

  • Hot, sunny climates — the white reflective surface helps buildings meet California Title 24 cool-roof requirements and reduces heat load on rooftop equipment
  • Large, open low-slope roofs — warehouses, distribution centers, retail, and manufacturing, where wide sheets and fast welding cover ground efficiently
  • Buildings where energy performance is a priority for the owner or required by code
  • Re-roof and new-construction budgets that need strong protection without the weight of a multi-ply system
  • Roofs with standard penetrations — TPO flashings weld cleanly around curbs, pipes, and drains
The honest part

Where TPO is not the right choice

No roof system is right for every building, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling a product, not a solution.

Over wet insulation

Installing any new single-ply — TPO included — over saturated insulation is never right. Trapped moisture destroys R-value and rots the assembly from the inside. If moisture surveys find wet material, it comes out first. There is no shortcut.

High-traffic or abuse-prone roofs

A single membrane layer means a single line of defense. Roofs with constant foot traffic, dropped tools, or heavy equipment service may be better served by a multi-ply system like BUR, or need walk pads and protection layers designed in from the start.

Chemical and grease exposure

Restaurant exhaust, industrial chemical discharge, and certain solvents can attack TPO membranes. If your roof sees this kind of exposure, tell your contractor — the answer may be a different membrane or specific protective detailing.

Manufacturers also vary in membrane formulation and thickness. A bargain-grade TPO and a premium TPO are not the same product — we'll show you the difference in the spec, not just the price.

Common questions

TPO roofing FAQ

How long does a TPO roof last?

A properly installed and maintained TPO system delivers decades of service. Actual lifespan depends on membrane thickness, climate exposure, rooftop traffic, and — above all — installation quality and ongoing maintenance.

Can TPO be installed over my existing roof?

Sometimes. Codes limit how many roof layers a building can carry, and any existing wet insulation must be removed first. We start with a moisture survey and core cuts before recommending overlay versus tear-off.

Is TPO the same as PVC?

No. Both are heat-welded thermoplastic single-plies, but they are different chemistries with different strengths — PVC generally handles grease and chemical exposure better, while TPO is often the more economical choice for standard commercial roofs. We'll recommend the one that fits your building.

Does a white roof really save energy?

In hot California and Nevada climates, a reflective roof reduces heat gain through the roof assembly and can lower cooling demand — it's why Title 24 pushes cool roofs on low-slope commercial buildings. Savings vary by building; we won't quote you a number we can't stand behind.

Insulated Metal Wall Panels

Factory-foamed panels that deliver structure, insulation, weather barrier, and finished architecture in a single pass — the fastest way to a high-performance building envelope.

The basics

What are insulated metal panels?

Insulated metal wall panels (IMPs) are factory-made sandwich panels: two metal skins bonded to a rigid foam core that is foamed in place at the factory under controlled conditions. Each panel arrives on site as a finished piece of wall — exterior finish, continuous insulation, air and water barrier, and interior liner in one component.

Installation is a single-pass envelope: panels interlock along concealed-fastener side joints and hang from the building's structural framing. Where a conventional wall assembly needs multiple trades installing studs, sheathing, insulation, barriers, and cladding in sequence, an IMP wall goes up in one trade, one pass.

Because the foam core is bonded to the skins in the factory rather than assembled in the field, the insulation is continuous and consistent — no gaps, no compressed batts, no field-quality variability inside the wall.

Best-use cases

Where IMPs excel

IMPs have become the default envelope for buildings where thermal performance and schedule both matter — and where a clean, modern exterior is part of the brief.

  • High insulation value per inch — the foam core outperforms most field-assembled wall insulation, with no thermal gaps
  • Fast enclosure — one trade, one pass gets the building dried in sooner, so interior trades start earlier and schedules compress
  • Cold storage, food processing, and temperature-controlled facilities, where envelope continuity is the whole game
  • Clean, modern aesthetics — flat and profiled faces, long panel lines, and a wide range of factory finishes and colors
  • Warehouses, manufacturing, and office/flex buildings that want performance and appearance from a single product
The honest part

Where IMPs are not the right choice

IMPs solve a lot of problems at once — but they are not a universal answer, and the details are unforgiving of sloppy work.

Joint quality decides everything

The panel is factory-perfect; the joints are field work. Sealant, gaskets, and flashing at every joint, opening, and transition determine whether the wall performs. An IMP wall installed by an inexperienced crew loses the very continuity you paid for.

Damage means panel replacement

A dented or forklift-struck panel usually can't be patched invisibly — the affected panel comes out. Buildings with high-impact exposure at grade may want protective bollards, liner panels, or a more repairable cladding in vulnerable zones.

Complex facades and retrofits

IMPs reward long, regular wall runs. Heavily articulated facades with many openings and geometry changes erode the speed advantage, and retrofitting IMPs onto an existing structure requires framing review — the structure has to accept the panel attachment loads and spans.

Fire and code requirements also vary by occupancy and jurisdiction — foam core types and assemblies carry specific listings, and the right panel for a warehouse isn't automatically right for every building. We spec to your code case, not from a brochure.

Common questions

Insulated metal panel FAQ

How do IMPs compare to conventional wall assemblies?

An IMP replaces several field-built layers — sheathing, insulation, air/water barrier, and cladding — with one factory-made component. That typically means faster enclosure and more consistent thermal performance; the trade-off is that joints and openings demand experienced detailing.

Can IMPs be used on an existing building?

Often, yes — recladding with IMPs can upgrade insulation and appearance in one move. It starts with a framing and attachment review to confirm the existing structure can carry the panels and meet current code. We assess that before quoting.

What maintenance do IMP walls need?

Less than most claddings, but not zero: periodic inspection of joints, sealants, and flashings, plus prompt attention to any impact damage. A simple preventative maintenance routine keeps the envelope performing and warranties intact.

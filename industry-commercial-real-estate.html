The challenge

The specific demands of CRE portfolios

Office parks, flex space, and mixed-use portfolios don't fail one roof at a time — they fail as line items in a budget you have to defend. The roofing partner has to think like an asset manager.

01

Portfolio-wide consistency

Different buildings, different vintages, different roof systems — but ownership expects one standard of workmanship, one reporting format, and one accountable point of contact across all of them.

02

Budget forecasting

Capital plans get built years ahead. You need honest remaining-life assessments per roof so replacements land in the right budget year — not as emergency line items that blow up NOI.

03

Tenant improvement coordination

TI projects cut new penetrations, add rooftop equipment, and can void roof warranties if the roofing contractor isn't in the loop. Every TI touching the roof needs coordinated detailing.

04

Lease-driven timelines

Roof condition affects lease-up, renewals, and dispositions. A documented, warrantied roof is a selling point in due diligence; a leaking one is a concession at the negotiating table.

05

Occupied Class A environments

Office tenants expect quiet, clean, and invisible. Odors in air intakes, noise over conference rooms, or debris in parking structures generate complaints that land on the property manager's desk.

06

Documentation for ownership

Institutional owners and lenders want condition reports, warranty files, and maintenance histories on demand. If it isn't documented, it didn't happen.

Systems

Roof systems we recommend for CRE assets

System selection should follow your hold strategy — a roof for a long-term core asset is a different decision than one for a value-add disposition. We install and maintain all of these.

TPO single-ply

The standard for modern office and flex product. Reflective membrane cuts cooling loads, installs quickly, and carries long-term manufacturer warranties that hold value at disposition.

EPDM single-ply

Decades of proven performance on low-slope commercial roofs. Flexible, durable, and straightforward to repair — a dependable choice across a mixed-vintage portfolio.

Built-up roofing (BUR)

Multi-ply redundancy for roofs with heavy mechanical traffic — a strong match for office assets where HVAC service crews are on the roof year-round.

Coatings & restoration

For occupied assets, fluid-applied restoration extends a serviceable roof's life without tear-off — often the highest-ROI move on a hold, deferring replacement into a planned budget year while keeping the asset watertight and warrantable.

Questions

CRE roofing FAQs

Can you assess our entire portfolio, not just one building?

Yes. We survey every roof to the same standard and deliver a consistent report per asset — system, age, condition, photos, remaining-life estimate, and recommended action — so you can rank spending across the portfolio instead of reacting building by building.

How do you help with multi-year capital planning?

From the portfolio assessment we build a sequenced roadmap: which roofs need repair now, which are restoration candidates, and which replacements belong in which budget year. You get defensible numbers for ownership before the budget cycle, not after a failure.

A tenant improvement project needs roof penetrations — can you coordinate?

Yes, and you should insist on it. We detail and flash TI penetrations to the roof manufacturer's specification and document the work, so tenant build-outs don't void the roof warranty or create leak paths that surface years later.

Will roofing work disrupt our office tenants?

Our job is to be invisible. We schedule loud or odor-producing work off-hours, coordinate with building engineers on air intakes and access, protect parking and entries, and communicate through your property management team so tenants hear one clear message.

Data Center Roofing

Mission-critical roofing for facilities where a single drop of water over live equipment is one drop too many.

The stakes

The specific demands of data center roofs

A data center roof isn't just a weather barrier — it's the last line of defense above millions of dollars of live equipment and the services that depend on it. That changes how the roof must be designed, maintained, and worked on.

  • Zero tolerance for leaks over energized equipment, server halls, and electrical rooms
  • Dense rooftop mechanical loads — HVAC, chillers, condensers — with heavy foot traffic around penetrations
  • Strict site security protocols: badged access, escorted crews, background screening, and controlled tool inventories
  • Uptime commitments that leave no room for unplanned disruption during roofing work
  • Heat management — reflective membranes and insulation choices that affect cooling loads
  • Redundancy expectations that extend to the building envelope, not just the systems inside it

Why it matters

On most buildings a slow leak is a maintenance ticket. Over a live data hall it's an incident. Roofing for mission-critical facilities means planning every cut, every tie-in, and every day of exposure around what's underneath.

Systems

Roof systems we recommend for data centers

The right system depends on your deck, rooftop equipment density, and cooling strategy — we recommend from the full range, not a single product line.

TPO Single-Ply

Heat-welded, highly reflective white membrane that helps manage rooftop heat gain — a strong fit for facilities where cooling load matters.

Built-Up (BUR)

Multi-ply redundancy: several waterproofing layers between the weather and your equipment, well suited to high-traffic roofs.

EPDM

Long-lived synthetic rubber membrane with reliable performance across wide temperature swings — proven on demanding low-slope roofs.

Concealed-fastener metal panels for long service life and minimal maintenance on sloped roof areas and architectural envelopes.

FAQ

Data center roofing questions

Can you roof over a live data hall without shutting anything down?

That's the goal of every plan we write. Work is phased so only a limited section is open at any time, each section is made watertight before crews leave, and interior protection is placed below the work area. Any activity that could affect operations is scheduled and approved with your facilities team first.

How do your crews handle site security requirements?

We work inside your protocols, not around them. Crew rosters are submitted in advance, personnel carry required badging, escorts are accommodated, and tools and materials are inventoried in and out. Because we self-perform, the people on your roof are Kodiak employees.

What happens if a leak appears over critical equipment?

Call 916.253.1900 — our emergency crews respond around the clock across California and Nevada. The first priority is containing water away from equipment; permanent repair follows once the space below is protected.

How do you protect rooftop HVAC and penetrations during work?

Every unit, curb, conduit, and penetration in the work area is mapped and covered under a written protection plan before tear-off begins. Flashings and terminations at equipment are detailed to manufacturer specification so the densest parts of the roof are also the most watertight.
